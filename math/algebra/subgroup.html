Subgroups

Introduction

Certain subsets of groups also form groups when you pass down the group operation. Such subsets are called subgroups, and we notate \(K ≤ G\). Mathematicians are so good at naming things!

To tell whether a subset \(K\) of a group \(G\) is a subgroup, you have to check a few things:

  1. \(K\) is closed under the group operation. That is to say, applying the group operation to elements of \(K\) just gets you back another element of \(K\).
  2. \(K\) is closed under taking inverses.
  3. The identity element is in \(K\).

If all three rules are satisfied, then \(K\) is a subgroup. For example, \(\mathbb{Z} ≤ \mathbb{Q} ≤ \mathbb{R}\) under addition.
